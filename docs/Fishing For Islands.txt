Overview
Fishing For Islands is a farming sim where you build your farm by pulling up Chunks of Land from the sea and adding them to your island. Unlike other farming games, the Chunks you discover will be different for each run, adding a roguelike component to the gameplay. While each Chunk has a standalone purpose (e.g. Wheat Farm, Sheep Grazing, Mine, Market), economic synergies exist for many combinations of Chunks (Sheep eat Wheat, gems from the Mine can be sold at Market, etc.). Players will (literally) build out their island over time, discovering new Chunks and new economic synergies as they play the game.


  
  

Left: Example of an early island. The player has discovered 7 Chunks, and has few synergies yet.
Right: Example of a midgame island. The player has discovered 21 Chunks, with several possible economic synergies. Additionally, mysterious locations (e.g. Faerie Woods) hint at more to come.


Why Will This Game Win?
The goal is to make a fun game that also pays the bills. Here’s the argument for why this will work:

* Popular Genres: Farming games continue to be popular on Steam, and cozy games overall are continuing to rise in popularity. Competition is high, but we can be confident the playerbase exists.

* Unique Visuals: Every other farming game starts with a farm that gradually gets refurbished over the course of the game. This flips that expectation on its head - you are literally adding land to your farm in a way no other game does. This helps us stand out in screenshots and get player attention.

* Surprise & Delight: Pulling an island from the sea when you don’t know what it is is exciting. It’s like opening up a present, and throughout each run you’ll open dozens of these delightful presents.

* Light Fantasy: Players of farming games love mystery and fantasy, provided that they are light (faeries, magic woods) and not heavy (fireballs, lightning bolts). This fits the “cottage core” visual aesthetic that is currently popular, and also helps us explain how you can pull chunks of land from the bottom of the sea (it’s magic - perhaps you are rediscovering the ruins of Atlantis?).

* Replayability: This is the biggest sales pitch differentiator. Your island will be different on every run, and you won’t be able to see all the Chunks in a single run. This is great for both replayability and for advertising; players who watch the game on Twitch still have a reason to play the game because they won’t have seen all (or even most!) of what the game has to offer.


Core Game Mechanics
The game takes place over multiple rounds (themed as days). At the start of the day, resources appear on each Chunk ready to be harvested. Once the player is done harvesting, planting, and processing all of the materials they’ve gained, they can head to their Camp to end the day. This resets resources and starts the daily cycle over again.

   * No Time Limit: Unlike other farming games, there’s no time limit - this is more cozy (like Animal Crossing) and less like a second job (like Stardew Valley).


Players will eventually have access to dozens if not hundreds of resources, items, and materials, but for now we’ll start with some basics (Wheat, Sheep, Wood, etc.).


Old Man (New Chunks)
The very first chunk you start on includes the mysterious Old Man. Functionally, he is the vendor for new chunks: you provide him with materials from your island, and he will give you special “bait” you can use to catch new Chunks of Land on your magical fishing hook.


   * Choice of Two: For now, the Old Man will offer a choice of two different options when he offers you “bait”. In the future I’ll determine the logic for how this works; some basic logic is below.


Flotsam (Daily Materials)
Since you’re on an island with a fishing hook, you should basically be able to fish from any edge of the island. Fishing in this way gives you flotsam - daily materials to help you get started. In this way, you can get a couple of pieces of Wood or a handful of Fish without needing a dedicated Chunk (such as Dense Forest) to get the materials.


   * Limited: You’ll be able to get only a handful of materials every day this way (3-5 items per day).


   * Random: Items are always determined from a random pool based upon your available Chunks. Essentially, each Chunk adds items to the list of items randomly available via Flotsam each day.


Tools (TBD)
The goal of the game is to have tools that open up new crafting recipes; for now, you can make anything if you have the correct Chunks and Items to do so. This should help us prove out the concept faster.




Chunk Order
Here is the order in which Chunks unlock:
   * Camp (Your Home) is always the first Chunk
   * After 1 Chunk, you unlock Wheat Farm (Chunk #2)
   * After 2 Chunks, you unlock Sheep Pasture and Bakery
   * After 3 Chunks, you unlock Dense Forest and Granary
   * After 4 Chunks, you unlock Cornfield and Fishing Pier
   * After 5 Chunks, you unlock Rocks, Market Square, and more


Your first choice is between Sheep Pasture and Bakery; after that, your options are randomized because you are limited to 2 choices at a time. Even with 10 Chunks, the game should feel different based on the options you pick; with more (50-100), there will be a tremendous number of playthroughs available.

Reason for Changes
Designer’s Note: My goal with these changes (and any future changes) is to find the fun as quickly as possible. I’m trying to trust my gut early to minimize the need to make more substantial changes later if and/or when we realize this game has legs.[a]


Here’s what my gut is telling me:
   * Islands are Cool. This is the really cool part, and it’s not on the market. There’s something here.
   * Mystery is Good. Even with AI generated text, I want to know more about the world. Go deeper.[b]
   * Goals are Missing. Both long-term and short-term goals. Why am I playing the game?
   * Short term, I’d like a fun loop for every day. What does a “level” look like in this game?
   * Long term, we need to advertise the mystery much faster (ideally from Day One).
   * Core Loop Feels Off. Obviously my “economy notes” from before had no weight to them, but I still think there needs to be a really strong loop every single day (as mentioned above). I don’t think it’s enough to have a simple economy that becomes complex - the hooks need to be there early on.


To paraphrase Margin Call (2011): Now that we know what the problem is, what can we do about it? My suggestions are detailed below.


Overview of Changes
In short, I’m making a lot of changes to how days work:
   * On day start, you discover a new island (you do this every day)
   * Every day, resources are refreshed and time advances (crops grow, sheep grow wool, etc.)
   * Guideline: Every day, you should see visual progress for anything that develops slowly
   * So Wheat goes from Planted → Sprouting → Growing → Flowering (Harvest Ready)
   * Every island has a story, and every story has multiple quests to progress through
   * Quests typically take one in-game day (the first quest always takes 1 day only)
   * You can progress one quest per story per day (but you can progress every story each day)
   * Think of these as Daily Quests from MMOs - you log in, play content, move on
   * Many of these stories specifically involve Crafting, so there’s a new Crafting Table
   * This makes up a lot of the early story - the Crafting Table is how you get situated
   * Completing Quest 1 of the story of the new island you just discovered advances time to sunset.
   * This is intended to be a visual effect - you can still do everything normally
   * But it’s a visual effect that sets up the end of day conversation (see below)
   * When you want to end the day, you go talk to the turtle pulling your big island
   * This is a new visual perspective! Normal game is top down, this is sideways
   * The reason it’s sideways is so you see the sunset that the Turtle is swimming towards
   * In the sun, you see the silhouette of the new island you’ll reach tomorrow
   * To end the day, you tell the Turtle your stories. This encourages the Turtle to swim on!
   * You always need the first quest of your newest island’s story
   * But you also tell the Turtle all the other stories you’ve experienced[c]


So the core loop is Start Day → Explore New Island → Solve New Island Quest → Talk to Turtle.


On each day, you’ll still be able to advance other quests in addition to the quest from your newest island - and you’ll want to in order to progress the overall story. But the core experience is “try something new every day”.[d] I think this will feel much better than the previous design did, where you might spend 2-3 in game days trying to grind up the requirements for a new island.


Island Spawning Changes
As before, the first few Islands (formerly Island Chunks, but let’s just call them Islands) come in a specific order. After that, though, the plan is for islands to spawn once prerequisites are met. This should make it easier to narratively connect islands together, since we’ll know which islands (and which characters) will be present whenever a given island appears.


Going forward, this should also give us an easy way to provide content forks for repeated plays. For example, we can say that on every run you get either Island X or Island Y, and therefore you would see different islands that use those islands as prerequisites. An example of this behavior is detailed below.


Story Changes (for context)
I want to have an idea where the story is going, even if we aren’t implementing it right away
   * No more Old Man - the Turtle is now the narrative gatekeeper for the entire story
   * The Turtle finds you adrift at sea and sets you ashore on an island (your home)
   * You then see a steady series of islands floating towards you, from the west
   * So - you keep the Turtle company as he swims west in search of answers


Narratively, the Turtle is powered by stories - as you tell him stories of the new islands you’ve helped out (i.e. quests you’ve completed), he gets excited and gets the energy he needs to swim faster and farther.
   * I’ll figure out how to wrap this into the major game plot, but I need to write it first 🙂
   * I guess you aren’t actually fishing for islands anymore. But I think it helps to see the new island coming towards you on the horizon. It’s like unwrapping a present - you’re guessing what it will be.[e]


There are also checkpoints that you swim towards. We aren’t implementing these right away, but the idea here is that you will find larger locations that serve as the “dungeons” of the game. They won’t be combat based, but they will offer exploration opportunities and reveal answers to the “floating islands” question
   * I’m envisioning four checkpoints right now corresponding to the Sun, Moon, Stars, and Sea.
   * Once 3 of 4 checkpoints are cleared, the player will begin the endgame. 
   * I haven’t designed this yet, but I’d expect you would stop finding new islands to give players time to finish the questlines of the most recent islands. It’s a countdown clock to the end!


None of this is really reflected in the core game mechanics detailed below - but again, I wanted to have a better idea for where we’re going so that there’s a clear directionality to everything we work on.


Game Pacing (for context)
One additional note - since every day brings a new island, we now have a game that is marching forward to a conclusion whether the player wants it or not. I think this is important for pacing - you don’t want the game to lose momentum and you want the player to feel like they’re always making progress.

Prerequisites for Drawing Islands
   * Current Day (Numbers): Prerequisite is met if the day is at least the current day.
   * Represented by numbers in circles, e.g. (5) = can draw starting on day 5
   * Previous Island (Arrows): Prerequisite is met if you already have one specific island unlocked

   * Red Arrows require all prerequisites, e.g. Auction House needs Bakery & Market Square

   * This will help us create branching paths for replayability
   * Randomness (Letters): On this run, you will draw either Island X or Island Y, but not both
   * This is decided purely by chance at the start of the run (at least for now)
   * For now, Island Z (Mount Mystery) will always appear once Gold Mine is on the board[f]

   * Seeds: For Wheat. Buried in the earth, you uncover them by digging.
   * The digging is a one time thing; you then get Seeds from Wheat yields
   * Wheat: Grows in 3 days. Planted (day 0) → Sprouting (d1) → Growing (d2) → Flowering (d3)
   * Harvesting a Wheat gets you… 1 Wheat. It’s simple.
   * You get 2-3 Seeds for every Wheat you harvest, so this field grows quickly!


Resource Spends
   * Time: It takes effort to plow the fields and plant the Wheat. Wheat grows in 3 days.
   * Seeds: Used to grow Wheat. Once you find it, plant it.
   * Water: Not much - one bucket of water will water all the seeds on the field


Quest 1: Play with Dog → Plant Wheat Seeds
   * Throw a stick at the dog! The dog will run… and not catch the stick.
   * Instead, the dog will dig up a hole and return to you with a Broken Spade
   * Use the Crafting Table to turn the Broken Spade into a Shovel 
   * Requirement: 2 Sticks, 3 Seaweed
   * NOTE: After you do this, you can also repair your Axe if you have Sticks (you’ll find more soon)
   * Requirement: 2 Sticks, 3 Seaweed (you may need to wait until tomorrow to get seaweed)
   * With Shovel in hand, go diffing on Wheat Isle - you’ll find a Bag of 10 Wheat Seeds and 5 Sticks
   * These are one time gifts - they don’t respawn
   * Plant the seeds in the holes you’ve dug and add Water from the well (1 bucket waters all seeds)
   * Quest and Day Complete!




 Quest 2: Play with Dog → Find Magic Wand (Day 3 or 4)
   * This quest unlocks the day you discover Wheat Farm, which is either Day 3 or Day 4
   * Throw a stick at the dog! The dog will run… and not catch the stick. Again.
   * Instead, the dog will dig up a hole and return to you with a Strange Stick
   * Show this to Sally (on Wheat Farm) and… POOF! You turn her hair from purple to brown
   * For now, the Magic Wand reverses prior decisions (in this case, hair dye)
   * But the plan is for the Magic Wand to let you turn Sticks back into Driftwood, etc.

Dense Forest (Day 5+)
Requirements: Day 5
Key source of Firm Wood, used for making and improving buildings.


Characters
   * Ashley: Rough and tumble lumberjack, happy to chat about woodcarvings
   * Squirrel: Always trying to find Acorns around the forest and hoard them


Tools & Stations
   * Logging Camp: used to turn Logs into Firm Wood


Resource Gains
   * Logs: Gained from Trees once you cut them down. Each tree grants 1 Log.
   * Trees grow in 4 days. Planted → Seedling → Sapling → Young Tree → Full Tree
   * You can only harvest Full Trees; you can’t cut them down early
   * The Dense Forest is dense, and starts with 20 Trees.
   * Firm Wood: Gained from Logs once you cut them further. Each Log grants 3 Firm Wood.
   * Acorns: Acorns drop from the Oak trees whenever they’re fully grown.
   * You get 1 Acorn per day per Full Tree on the board at day start
   * Half of these Acorns are on the Dense Forest; half are found on the other islands
   * Acorns just sort of blow everywhere. This is an early way to affect all islands
   * You also get 1 Acorn every time you cut down a Full Tree


Resource Spends
   * Time: It takes time to cut down Trees, and time for them to regrow
   * Water: One bucket of water will water up to 5 planted Acorns - they take more water than Wheat!
   * As with Wheat, you only need to water them when you plant them


Quest 1: Learn to Log with Ashley
   * Meet Ashley and the Squirrel. Squirrel is suspicious and covetous, Ashley is chill.
   * Requirement: Cut up 5 Logs (this will gain you 15 Wood)
   * You get to keep all of the wood; Ashley just wants to see if you can do it.
   * She values strength and action, and wants to see what you’re made of
   * Quest and Day Complete!




 Quest 2: Storing Up Acorns
   * Longer term quest that involves the Squirrel, with Ashley providing color commentary.
   * The Squirrel wants to save up Acorns for the winter… whenever that happens. Lots of Acorns.
   * Requirement: Give 100 Acorns to the Squirrel
   * Note: You can give any number of Acorns to the Squirrel, he’s happy to bank them for you
   * Quest Complete! But the day isn’t over yet.




 Quest 3: Winter is Here
   * This quest triggers 3 days after you finish Quest 2. Winter comes - the Squirrel was correct!
   * There’s a great big TODO about what happens when winter comes - for now, it’s cosmetic
   * Notably, there should be snow everywhere, and snow should be falling as well
   * The ocean doesn’t freeze, though - too much water, and it’s not quite that cold
   * Talking to the Squirrel will result in the Squirrel (with Ashley’s help) asking you to make a Snowman
   * Not a real craft, you’ll just make one on the ground
   * Once you do, the Squirrel topples the Snowman… revealing a powerful magical Frost Stone.
   * The intent is to slowly reveal more magic and mystery as the game continues on.
   * Quest Complete! But the day isn’t over yet, and winter continues until tomorrow.
   * Presumably you’ll use the Frost Stone to trigger winter, but I haven’t designed that yet.

 Quest 2: Dancing for Pearls
   * This quest continues for many days. Each day, you bring in 3 Fish and get a Pristine Shell.
   * But… every day, Timmy will tell you that what the Seals really want is X (a certain type of fish). If you give Timmy 3 Fish of the specific type, you’ll receive a Pearl - a valuable item.
   * First Day: Bring Timmy 3 Halibut
   * Second Day: Bring Timmy 3 Herring
   * Third Day: Bring Timmy 3 Snapper
   * Fourth Day: Bring Timmy 3 Tuna
   * Fifth Day: Bring Timmy 3 Flounder

   * NOTE: After you complete this quest, the Fish for Pearls action continues.

   * He’ll also say “but if you want to give them a super rare treat, go ahead!”




 Quest 3: The Dance of the Depths
   * This is a hidden quest, and activates whenever the condition is met.
   * The condition is to give Timmy (and the Seals) 3 Golden Trout. You know, the extremely rare fish.
   * Upon receiving such a gift, the Seals do not dance. Instead, they swim next to shore - next to you.
   * When you go to the Seals, they pull you into the ocean depths, far away from the sun’s light.
   * In the dark of the ocean’s abyss, you will see a vision - an ethereal queen, filled with power.
   * She tells you her name is Sen, and that you have been blessed by the darkness.
   * Then you emerge on shore, with a powerful magical Shadow Stone in your possession.
   * As with the Flame and Frost stones, details here are TODO.
   * Quest Complete! But the day isn’t over yet.
